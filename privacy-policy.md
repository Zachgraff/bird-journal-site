# Bird Journal — Privacy Policy

**Effective date:** 14 October 2026
**Applies to:** the Bird Journal iOS app (bundle id `com.zachgraff.birdjournal`)

## The short version

Bird Journal does not collect your data. There is no account, no server, and
nothing in the app sends anything about you anywhere. Your photos, the names
you give your birds, and the places you spotted them live only on your phone.

## What the app stores, and where

Everything you create in Bird Journal is saved in the app's own storage on your
device:

- **Photos** you take with the in-app camera.
- **Sightings**: when you saved it, the bird's name if you chose one, and when
  you first named it. Nothing else is written about a sighting.
- **Locations**, only if you allow location access. The app records the place
  of a sighting after you take the photo, never before, and never in the
  background. You can decline and the app works the same.
- **Your momentum**: which days you went out and which days you did a bird
  lesson. These only ever fill in; nothing counts streaks or gaps.
- **Reminder settings**, if you choose to turn on a daily reminder.

None of this is uploaded, synced to us, or shared with anyone. We never see it.

## Backups are yours

If your iPhone backs up to iCloud or to a computer, Apple's backup may include
the app's data, exactly like any other app on your phone. That backup is yours
and Apple's; it is not something we run or can read. You control it in your
iPhone settings.

## The map

The personal map shows your sightings on Apple Maps. To draw the map, your
phone asks Apple for map tiles for the area on screen. That request comes from
the operating system and contains the map viewport, not your sightings, photos
or identity. We do not add any map provider or API key of our own.

## Reminders

Daily reminders are optional and off by default. If you turn them on, the
reminder is scheduled on your phone by the operating system. There is no push
notification service and no device token is sent anywhere.

## Permissions the app asks for

- **Camera** — to take bird photos. Asked when you first open the camera and
  tap Enable camera, not before.
- **Location (while using the app)** — to remember where you spotted a bird.
  Asked after your first photo, never on launch, and never "always".
- **Notifications** — only if you choose to turn on reminders.

The app never asks for the microphone, contacts, photo library, or anything
else.

## Analytics, ads, and third parties

There are none. No analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising, no tracking,
no social features, no third-party SDKs that send data. The app makes no
network calls of its own.

## Bird photos in the app

The bird pictures used in the species picker, species pages, and lessons are
not yours; they come from Wikimedia Commons under licenses that allow sharing
with credit. Every photographer is credited on the app's **Photo credits**
screen, and tapping a credit opens the original page in your browser. Tapping a
credit, or the Privacy policy link on that screen, are the only times the app
hands a web address to your browser, and both addresses are built into the app,
never derived from your data.

## Children and younger users

Bird Journal is made for people aged 16 and up but collects nothing from
anyone, of any age. There is no sign-up and no age gate because there is no
data to gate.

## Deleting your data

Delete the app and everything it stored goes with it. You can also delete
individual sightings inside the app. Because we never had a copy, there is
nothing for us to delete on our side.

## If this policy ever changes

If a future version of Bird Journal ever collected or sent any data, this
policy would say so plainly, the app would ask you first, and the change would
be listed in the App Store release notes. Today it collects nothing.
